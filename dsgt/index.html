Unit II — Mathematical Relations

Rank 1 · 6 questions

Relations run this paper. Equivalence, Warshall's transitive closure, and Hasse diagrams with lattices fill Q1a, Q3a and Q3b in both papers — three fixed slots, six questions between them.

Transitive closure / Warshall

2 / 2 papers 8 marks both times
Dec 2025 · Q3a · 8 marks
A Data Science program has a prerequisite network among modules: M1: Python Basics; M2: DS Foundation; M3: Machine Learning; M4: Deep Learning; M5: Deployment. Given relation R = {(1,2), (2,3), (3,4), (4,5), (1,1), (3,3), (5,5)}. i. Apply Warshall's Algorithm to compute transitive closure. ii. For each Mi, list all modules it can unlock. iii. Identify root modules and terminal modules. iv. Explain how this helps curriculum planners maintain valid learning paths.
May 2026 · Q3a · 8 marks
A domestic airline company maintains a directed flight network among four cities: V = {a, b, c, d}. The direct flight connections are represented by the relation: R = {(a,b), (b,d), (d,c), (d,a)}. The company wants to analyze all possible direct and indirect travel routes, including circular journeys. Based on the given flight network: 1. Identify and explain the concept of reachability / transitive closure used in the graph. 2. Determine all possible ordered pairs of cities connected through: direct routes, routes with layovers, and circular routes. 3. Analyze how graph connectivity helps optimize airline route planning. 4. Represent the final connectivity relation for the graph.

Hasse diagram / lattice

2 / 2 papers 7 marks both times
Dec 2025 · Q3b · 7 marks
Data storage providers offer plans of sizes: S = {1 TB, 2 TB, 4 TB, 8 TB, 16 TB}. Relation a | b (a divides b). i. Draw the Hasse diagram. ii. List all (a,b) where a divides b. iii. Check whether S forms a lattice (meet & join for every pair). iv. Explain impact on designing upgrade/downgrade recommendations and plan combinations in the storage app.
May 2026 · Q3b · 7 marks
A cloud access-control system organizes permission levels using divisibility-based hierarchy. The permission levels are: A = {1,2,3,6,12,24,36,72}. A relation R is defined on A as: (x,y) ∈ R if x | y. Based on this hierarchy: 1. Write all ordered pairs of relation R. 2. Construct the Hasse Diagram of the poset. 3. Analyze whether the given poset forms a lattice. 4. Justify your answer using suitable meet and join examples.

Equivalence relation

2 / 2 papers 5 marks both times
Dec 2025 · Q1a · 5 marks
A student-feedback system groups teacher responses using the rule: Two responses x and y are "similar" if they fall in the same satisfaction band (e.g., 1–2 = Low, 3 = Medium, 4–5 = High). i. Using definitions of reflexive, symmetric, transitive, determine whether "similarity" is an equivalence relation. ii. Explain the classroom-level meaning of each property. iii. Discuss how violating any property affects AI-based feedback analytics and dashboard reports.
May 2026 · Q1a · 5 marks
A digital geometry learning platform groups polygons based on the number of sides for automatic classification and retrieval. Let A be the set of all polygons. A relation R is defined on A as: R = {(P1, P2): P1 and P2 have the same number of sides}. Based on this classification system: 1. Analyze whether the relation R is an equivalence relation on A. 2. Verify the properties of reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity. 3. Construct suitable equivalence classes for triangles, quadrilaterals, and pentagons. 4. Interpret how equivalence relations help organize geometric data in digital systems.

Unit IV — Counting

Rank 1 · 6 questions

Counting pays in every paper. Inclusion–exclusion and recurrence are full 8- and 7-mark questions, and the pigeonhole principle is a fixed Q1 short. Three topics, all asked in both papers.

Inclusion–exclusion

2 / 2 papers 8 marks both times
Dec 2025 · Q2a · 8 marks
A campus event app records students who use three app sections: E: Event Announcements; C: Clubs & Communities; R: Reels / Short videos. Survey data table: E 140; C 110; R 120; E∩C 65; E∩R 70; C∩R 55; Total Students 200. i. Using inclusion–exclusion, compute how many students use all three sections. ii. Find students who use only one section. iii. Explain how this analysis helps app developers and event committees design targeted notifications and UI improvements.
May 2026 · Q2a · 8 marks
A pharmaceutical company conducts a drug trial on 1000 people to analyze the side effects of a newly developed medicine. The following observations are recorded: 122 people develop symptom A; 88 people develop symptom B; 112 people develop symptom C; 27 people develop both symptoms A and B; 29 people develop both symptoms A and C; 32 people develop both symptoms B and C; 10 people develop all three symptoms A, B, and C. Using the Principle of Inclusion and Exclusion: 1. Analyze how many people develop at least one symptom. 2. Determine the number of people who show exactly one symptom. 3. Construct a Venn diagram and fill all regions appropriately.

Recurrence relation

2 / 2 papers 7 marks both times
Dec 2025 · Q5b · 7 marks
A student club records followers using recurrence: Fn = 4Fn−1 − 4Fn−2. With, F0 = 10 (core team); F1 = 30 (after first promotion). i. Solve the recurrence relation. ii. Describe growth pattern (linear/exponential). iii. Comment whether such growth is realistic for a campus club in long term.
May 2026 · Q5b · 7 marks
A financial analytics company models the quarterly growth of a digital investment portfolio using a recurrence relation. Let Fn represent the portfolio value during the nth quarter. The growth pattern is governed by: Fn = 3Fn−1 + 10Fn−2 + 7·5n, with initial conditions: F0 = 4, F1 = −3. Based on this predictive model: 1. Solve the given recurrence relation using an appropriate method. 2. Determine the general expression for Fn. 3. Analyze the effect of the exponential forcing term 7·5n on the sequence growth. 4. Interpret how recurrence relations are useful in financial forecasting and computational modeling.

Mathematical induction

A student deposits savings in a fund according to: Daily saving: Sn = 4n − 1. They want to verify if Sn is always odd for all natural numbers n. i. Prove the statement using mathematical induction. ii. Explain the meaning for planning weekly budgets and long-term savings.
May 2026 · Q4b · 7 marks
A data analyst observes that the cumulative computational cost of a repeated process is modeled by the series: Σr=1n r(r + 3). The analyst claims that for every positive integer n, Σr=1n r(r + 3) = (1/3)n(n + 1)(n + 5), n ≥ 1, n ∈ N. Using the principle of mathematical induction: 1. Verify the result for the base case. 2. Prove the inductive step. 3. Conclude that the formula is valid for all n ≥ 1. 4. Interpret how induction helps validate formulas in algorithm analysis.

Logical equivalence

The transport officer rewrites: (P ∨ Q) → R ≡ (P → R) ∧ (Q → R). i. Prove the above using logical equivalence laws step-by-step. ii. Explain how this version ensures no boarding method is ignored, improves data consistency, and reduces false "absent" cases.
May 2026 · Q1b · 5 marks
A software security system uses logical rules to decide whether access should be granted, denied, or forwarded for further verification. Consider the following logical expressions: ((p → q) → r) and (p ∧ ¬q) ∨ r. Using a series of logical equivalences, prove that the two expressions are logically equivalent. Graph isomorphism

2 / 2 papers 8 marks both times
Dec 2025 · Q5a · 8 marks
Two lab surveillance networks are modeled as undirected graphs: Graph G1 (Cameras A,B,C,D): A: B, C; B: A, D; C: A, D; D: B, C. Graph G2 (Cameras P,Q,R,S): P: Q, R; Q: P, S; R: P, S; S: Q, R. i. Determine whether G1 and G2 are isomorphic. ii. If yes, provide the mapping. iii. Explain how detecting isomorphic patterns helps in reusing surveillance layouts and minimizing storage of duplicate designs.
May 2026 · Q5a · 8 marks
A computer networking company proposes two communication network layouts represented by graphs G1 and G2. The organization wants to verify whether both network structures provide the same connectivity pattern even though the device labels are different. The graphs are represented as: V(G1) = {A,B,C,X,Y,Z}; E(G1) = {(A,X), (A,Y), (B,X), (B,Y), (C,Y)}; V(G2) = {1,2,3,4,5,6}; E(G2) = {(1,4), (1,5), (2,4), (2,5), (3,5)}. Based on the above network structures: 1. Define isomorphism of graphs. 2. Analyze whether graphs G1 and G2 are isomorphic. 3. If they are isomorphic, determine a valid one-to-one correspondence between the vertices. 4. Justify your answer by comparing degree sequence and adjacency preservation.

How to pass — for exam tension

Minimum study · target 30+ / 60

The plan below puts 45 marks on the table from just Q1 plus Q2 plus Q3 — so even with mistakes, you stay above the pass band. Add one more full question and it becomes 60 marks on the table.

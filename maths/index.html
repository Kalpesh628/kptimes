🎯 Attachment 1: How to pass — for exam tension

Minimum study · target 30+ / 60

Breathe. You do not need all 6 units, and you do not need 60 out of 60. Passing lives around 27–30 marks out of 60. The plan below puts 35 marks on the table from just the Q1 short skills plus four full-question topics — so even with mistakes, you stay above the pass band. Add the safe set and it becomes 51 marks on the table.

Step 1 — the minimum set (learn these first, in this order)

LearnWhere it paysMarks on the table
Q1 short skills — eigenvalues shortcut, LPP standard form / dual, Fourier an, MGF / moments, remainder, SpearmanQ1, any 515
Poisson distributionQ2a4
Karl Pearson correlationQ2b5
Chinese Remainder TheoremQ3b5
Fourier expansion (x² in (0, 2) is the repeat)Q2c6
Total on the table35

Step 2 — the safe set (add these and you cover 51 marks)

Eigenvalues & eigenvectors full question (6) + Cayley–Hamilton (4) + Lagrange multipliers (6) = 16 more marks. 35 + 16 = 51 out of 60 of the paper's usual shape.

Step 3 — how to attempt in the hall (2 hours)

Q1 first (25 min) — the six short skills are the most predictable marks in the paper. Then Q2 (30 min), then Q3 (30 min) — both are templated, see the model paper below. Then your strongest remaining question (25 min), last 10 min to check. Attempt every part you touch, even half an answer — step marks add up, a blank scores zero. If a slot surprises you, fall back to the safe set instead of freezing on one question.

Honest note, not a guarantee. This pattern comes from only two papers, and MU can break a slot any year. Nothing here guarantees a pass — but it is the highest-coverage, lowest-effort path the evidence supports: 35 marks on the table from the minimum set, 51 with the safe set, against a 27–30 pass band.
